VIThe method

How we count.

Blamed on AI
Challenger, Gray & Christmas tallies announced U.S. job cuts every month and records the reason employers give. This is their “artificial intelligence” figure. It counts announcements and stated reasons — it does not measure what AI actually did.
Meets the bar
At the strictest setting — the number we publish — a layoff counts only when the company itself, in an SEC filing, a public memo, or an official statement, named AI as a reason, and the headcount is documented. Loosening the bar adds our inferences, then the headlines, so you can watch the number inflate. Even the strictest figure is a claim; it is simply the strongest one available.
Unverified
Blamed minus what meets the bar. Not “false”: it is blame nobody has put their name to. Some of it is real and undocumented; some is ordinary cuts dressed up as AI; some is headlines. We can't tell which from here — so we don't pretend to.
All layoffs
The Bureau of Labor Statistics' JOLTS survey: layoffs and discharges, seasonally adjusted. It counts how many jobs end each month. It never says why.

What this can't tell you

  • Our ledger is hand-researched, not exhaustive. It covers major, named layoffs and skews toward tech and large firms. Part of the gap is coverage we haven't reached yet.
  • Two different yardsticks. Challenger counts announcements; JOLTS counts separations. We show them side by side for scale and never add them together.
  • A company saying “AI” is still a claim. Firms have reasons to say it — and reasons not to. We record what they said, not what is true.
  • The layoffs are real either way. Whatever the cause, every number on this page is people.
